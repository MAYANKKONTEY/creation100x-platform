# Hook types

| Type | Mechanism | Pattern | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | Challenges a common belief | "{Common advice} is wrong for {audience}." | Straw man |
| Controversial but defensible | Takes a side in a real debate | "{Position}. Here's the evidence." | Outrage drift. Must be backed by evidence. |
| Curiosity | Opens a gap the post closes | "{Unexpected thing} happened when {situation}." | Withholding too long |
| Unexpected insight | Non-obvious connection | "{A} and {B} have the same problem." | Forced analogy |
| Data-driven | Specific sourced number | "{Number} {metric} — {implication}." | Unsourced or cherry-picked |
| Problem-focused | Names a pain precisely | "If {symptom}, the problem isn't {usual blame}." | Vague pain |
| Storytelling | Scene or moment | "{Time}. {Situation}. {Tension}." | Fabricated story |
| Comparison | Two options side by side | "{X} vs {Y}: {surprising axis}." | False dichotomy |
| Question | Asks something the reader can't ignore | "Why does {thing} still {problem}?" | Generic "What do you think?" |
| Strong opinion | Clear stance, owned | "I think {stance}." | Opinion not actually held by owner. Check voice profile. |

## Scoring rubric (0–10)
- **Clarity:** can a stranger tell the subject in one read?
- **Relevance:** does it matter to engineers, AI builders, and founders?
- **Curiosity:** does it create a reason to keep reading without deceiving?
- **Specificity:** concrete nouns, numbers, names rather than abstractions.
- **Credibility:** is it supported by the brief and believable from this author?
- **Originality:** not a template phrase seen a thousand times.
